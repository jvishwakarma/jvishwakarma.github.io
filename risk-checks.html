TIMELINE
1 Month · 2026

The last gate before an account opens

After a business submits its application, staff screen the company and its people against sanctions and watchlist data from World-Check. Each application can return several hits.

Most are false positives: a shared name, a common city. But each one needs a recorded decision, and a single missed true match is a regulatory failure. Slow reviews, meanwhile, leave a customer waiting days for an account.

THE PROBLEM

Five hits, one decision, zero room for error.

Sessions with reviewers surfaced three needs the screen had to meet.

01

"Where am I?"

Reviewers jump between applications all day. They need to see instantly how many hits are left and what they already decided.

02

"Why did this match?"

A hit is only as useful as its reason. Name, place and date-of-birth matches carry very different weight.

03

"Can I prove it later?"

Approving with an open hit, or over a true match, must be impossible, and every outcome needs a reason an auditor can read.

KEY DECISIONS

From checklist to guided review.

01 — Progress at a glance

One strip answers "how much is left?"

Resolved count, a progress bar and a tally of true matches, false positives and pending hits sit above the list. The pending count also badges the side navigation, so a returning reviewer knows the state in one look.

02 — One hit in focus

Decide, and the next pending hit opens

Each hit leads with "Matched on" tags, so the reason for the match is read first. A call collapses the card and opens the next pending hit, and a "Next pending hit" shortcut jumps straight there.

03 — Approval that explains itself

A locked button that tells you why

Approve stays disabled until every hit is resolved and none is a true match. Instead of a silent grey button, a line underneath states exactly what's missing. Decline and Return to sourcing stay available throughout.

04 — Every call on the record

The reasoning lives next to the decision

The decision panel pairs the overall outcome with a required comment and an evidence upload, saved straight to the audit trail. It mirrors the hit list ("3 hits still pending, outcome is provisional") so nobody commits to an outcome the evidence doesn't support yet.

DESIGN SYSTEM

Built on the bank's corporate UI

Every element maps to the existing corporate-ui library (built on MUI), so engineering shipped with components they already maintain.

  • —Secondary, destructive and affirmative buttons for the three outcomes
  • —Brand colour tokens and the web type scale
  • —Core spacing scale from 2 to 80 px
ACCESSIBILITY

Usable by every reviewer

A tool used all day by compliance staff has to work with a keyboard and a screen reader, not just a mouse.

  • —Status never relies on colour alone: every chip has a text label
  • —44 px minimum targets on decision buttons
  • —Expandable hits and toggle decisions announce their state
